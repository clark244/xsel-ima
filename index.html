Key Findings

Evidence at both ends, nothing in the middle
Three federal measurement awards and ten peer-reviewed papers establish that SELweb measures what it claims, and that the skills it measures predict classroom behavior and academic achievement. Nothing establishes what happens in between — whether teachers open the reports, understand them, and change instruction.
The instrument is not the intervention
What would have to be evaluated is not SELweb but whether and how educators use the assessment reports, whether and how much that influences instruction, and whether those instructional decisions in turn influence teacher and student success.
The organization has been responsive to feedback, when articulated
In response to customer feedback, the team shortened the assessment, shelved a subtest, and addressed objections. A teacher who opens a report, understands nothing, and changes nothing generates no feedback, which is why the impact of SELweb reports on instruction and outcomes has stayed invisible.
Two of the three inputs for a credible study are in hand
The comparison variation exists inside the customer base, and the outcome measure exists in-house. What is missing is measures of teacher behavior between fall and spring assessments, and because report access has not been logged, there is no retrospective route to it.

About xSEL Labs

xSEL Labs offers SELweb, a direct assessment of children's social-emotional skills in three grade-banded editions: children complete the tasks themselves rather than being rated by a teacher. Districts buy it; teachers receive norm-referenced reports on their classes and individual students, and curriculum crosswalks and professional development are intended to turn those reports into changed instruction. This assessment covers that chain. The 2023 acquisition and the peer-rejection screener, built under the original grant and deliberately shelved, are outside its scope.

Model Description

ElementDescription
ProductSELweb — a web-based, performance-based benchmark assessment of social and emotional skills, in three editions: Early Elementary (K–3), Late Elementary (4–6), and Middle School (6–8). Four skills are measured: emotion recognition, perspective-taking, social problem-solving, and self-regulation.
Implementation ContextWhole-class administration during the school day once in the fall and once in the spring. Students work independently with headphones, and instructions are embedded in the assessment so that non-readers can complete it. Roughly 30 minutes. Results are reported against national norms at student, classroom, grade, school, and district level.
User / Population ContextK–8 classroom teachers, principals, and district administrators. Purchase decisions are typically made by heads of student services or of curriculum and instruction. Districts run established SEL curricula, with at least a dozen crosswalked to SELweb.
User BehaviorThe behavior the model depends on: a teacher reviews the class report, uses the curriculum crosswalk to locate the units that teach the weakest skill, and adjusts unit emphasis, pre-teaches, re-teaches, or coaches skills — without breaking the curriculum's scope and sequence.
Mechanism 1How report review, the crosswalk, and professional development combine to identify a specific instructional target. This is the step that converts a score into a lesson decision. It has no operational definition today, and the crosswalk — the artifact that does the converting — is distributed by request with no record of who receives it.
Mechanism 2How targeted skill instruction develops the four measured skills. The underlying claim is well supported in the SEL literature and in xSEL Labs' own criterion work, but it has not been observed among SELweb users, because the instructional change upstream of it has not been measured.
Intended OutcomeImproved student social and emotional skills, and through them better peer relationships, less conflict-driven disruption, recovered instructional time, and stronger academic outcomes. xSEL Labs' own research establishes that SELweb scores are associated with classroom behavior and academic achievement across all three editions.

Evidence Landscape

Before identifying measurement opportunities, it is worth cataloging the evidence xSEL Labs already has. This inventory matters here because the portfolio is unusually strong and unusually lopsided — the priorities that follow are gap-filling against a substantial base, not a starting point.

Evidence AssetWhat It ShowsLimitations
Three IES measurement awards, 2011–2024SourceR305A110143 ($2,325,731); R305A160053 ($1,399,997); R305A200220 ($1,398,404)
 Thirteen consecutive years of federal measurement funding, with McKown as principal investigator throughout. Each award produced a validated instrument and peer-reviewed publications, delivered on schedule. All three are Measurement-goal awards funding assessment development and validation, not whether using the assessment leads to improved instruction and student outcomes.
Peer-reviewed psychometric corpusSourceTen papers, 2016–2025, in Psychological Assessment, Assessment, Social Development, Autism Research, Journal of Psychoeducational Assessment, and others
 Score reliability, factor structure, and item-level performance for all three editions. Measurement invariance across gender, ethnicity, time, and English and Spanish — meaning a score carries the same meaning for different groups of students, which most SEL measures cannot demonstrate. Addresses the properties of the instrument. No paper examines what educators do with the results.
Criterion validity evidenceSourceReported within the EE, LE, and MS validation papers, and in McKown, Russo-Ponsaran, Allen & Warren (2016), Infant and Child Development, 25, 119–136
 SELweb scores are associated with behavioral and academic outcomes across all three editions. The association with reading achievement holds controlling for IQ and is mediated by positive classroom behavior. Establishes that the skills matter. Does not establish that assessing them changes instruction, which is the step in between.
Shortened SELweb EE — the shipping formSourceMcKown, Kharitonova, Russo-Ponsaran & Aksu-Dunya (2023), Assessment, 30, 171–189
 A shortened form that saves substantial administration time for a modest cost in score reliability. It is a documented answer to the objection districts actually raise — whether teachers will think it is worth the time. The Spanish adaptation, the autism-population study, and the two full-length EE papers validated the predecessor instrument rather than the shipping form.
Special-population and Spanish-language validationsSourceRusso-Ponsaran, Lerner, McKown et al. (2019), Autism Research, 12, 1260–1271; Russo, McKown, Russo-Ponsaran & Allen (2018), Psychological Assessment, 30, 416–421
 Validity evidence in high-functioning autistic elementary students and in a Spanish-language adaptation — reach into two populations that most SEL measures do not address at all. Neither population appears in the product's positioning, case studies, or sales materials.
District case studiesSourceFive published studies: LAUSD (Spring 2022), Lancaster SD, Distinctive Schools, Mineola UFSD, and Glencoe District 35
 Documented adoption and implementation across varied district contexts, and one professional learning community at LAUSD using report data to guide SEL practice — the closest existing example of the intended teacher behavior. Four of the five are adoption narratives. The LAUSD outcome claim is an uncontrolled pre/post on xSEL Labs' own instrument, inside a highly resourced professional learning community.
Platform login dataSourceSELweb platform — most-recent-login field
 Confirms that accounts are accessed. A single mutable field, not an event log. There is no record of report views, time spent, or crosswalk requests, and no history to analyze retrospectively. Every question downstream of login requires additional data.

Two patterns emerge. First, the evidence is concentrated at the two ends of the causal chain and absent in the middle: the instrument is validated, the skills are shown to predict outcomes, and nothing describes what teachers do in between. Second, several genuine assets are not in circulation — the shipping form's own validation, two special-population studies, and the most recent LE and MS papers are missing from the public evidence page. The priorities that follow address the first pattern; the second is a communication fix and the cheapest item in this report.

Measurement Opportunities

A central finding of this analysis is that xSEL Labs faces one measurement problem rather than several, and that it sits in a specific place: between the moment a teacher opens a report and the moment instruction changes. Everything upstream of that moment is well evidenced. Everything downstream depends on it.

That problem has an unusual property. Because report access has never been logged beyond a single most-recent-login field, and because crosswalk requests are not recorded either, there is no retrospective route to it. Every route to an answer begins with new collection — and the fastest of those routes is not the platform, it is asking teachers directly.

The Three Priorities

The priorities are ordered by dependency, not by schedule. Priorities 1 and 2 begin immediately and run alongside each other; Priority 3 is gated on Priority 1 and delivers later.

1 Define the Intervention — What Is Actually Being Evaluated Now ○ ○ ● ○ ▼
Start Here

SELweb is an assessment. The goal of SELweb is to support effective teaching and student learning, which is an intervention goal. The first goal, therefore, is to specify the chain of measurable events that have to happen, in what order, and in what manner for the fall assessment reports to influence teaching and learning.

What to measure

  • A written description of the composite intervention: what the teacher receives, what the teacher is expected to do, and in what sequence
  • A dosage definition — how often report review and instructional adjustment must occur for the intervention to count as delivered
  • A fidelity standard specifying the difference between delivered and not delivered, usable by an observer
  • A decision on whether the crosswalk is part of the intervention or a support material — the founder's account says the former, the product treats it as the latter

This is a specification exercise rather than a data exercise, and it is inexpensive. It comes first because it determines what Priority 2 asks and what Priority 3 logs, and because logging is now an engineering build rather than a database query, specifying it correctly the first time is worth real money. The LAUSD professional learning community is the nearest existing example of what a defined, dosable version looks like in practice.

2 Teacher Understanding and Instructional Change — Ask Teachers Now Now ● ● ● ○ ▼
Highest Value

This is the visibility gap. Asking teachers has no engineering dependency and can begin this school year, which makes it the only priority that returns evidence on the current timeline.

Three things have to be established. First, whether the teacher received a crosswalk for their curriculum — a teacher without one cannot have failed at the application step. Second, whether the teacher can read the report correctly, checked against their own class's scores rather than by asking how confident they feel. Third, what specifically changed, named against the curriculum rather than answered yes or no.

What to measure

  • Crosswalk receipt — which teachers have one, for which curriculum, and how it reached them
  • Report comprehension — a short check against the teacher's own class data, not a self-rating of confidence
  • Specific instructional change — units re-sequenced, skills re-taught, real-time coaching, named against the curriculum
  • Non-users and lapsed users, sampled deliberately alongside engaged teachers

One design caution belongs in the scope from the start. The threat here is not measurement error; it is that teachers who received professional development and opened a report will report having used it. Self-report on professional practice runs warm. That argues for anchoring every claim to a specific artifact or event the teacher can name, and for a small observation or artifact-review component to calibrate the self-report. This is implementation-research craft rather than psychometrics, and it is the capability worth adding to an already strong measurement team.

3 Report and Crosswalk Telemetry to Make the Answer Continuous 6–18 months ○ ○ ● ● ▼
Durable Infrastructure

The platform currently stores a single most-recent-login field. It does not record whether a report was opened, which levels or sections were viewed, how long was spent, or whether a crosswalk was ever requested. Building that record converts a one-time study into standing data: Priority 2 answers the question once for a sample, and Priority 3 answers it continuously for the entire customer base.

What to measure

  • Report view events — timestamp, level viewed, and section-level interaction
  • Crosswalk distribution — who requests, for which curriculum, and whether it reaches classroom teachers or stops with administrators
  • A short in-platform prompt after report view, asking what was used and what changed, joined to professional development attendance

Priority 3 is the only item here that is a product roadmap negotiation rather than a research commission, which makes it the one most likely to slip; it should carry a named owner and a date. It is also worth noting that xSEL Labs has never logged this and has therefore never been misled by it. There are no legacy engagement metrics to unwind, and the logging can be specified from the causal model outward rather than inherited from whatever was instrumented first.

Recommended Next Steps

The three priorities imply a specific set of near-term actions. The first three can begin immediately and require no engineering. The last two prepare the platform work so that it is specified before it is built.

Immediate

1
Draft the intervention specification. Name the composite — assessment, crosswalk, professional development, instructional adjustment — and define its dosage and fidelity. One document, and everything else depends on it.
2
Update the public evidence page. It lists five papers through 2019; the record is ten through 2025. A district evaluating SELweb LE or MS today finds no validation evidence for the instrument it is being sold. This is an afternoon's work and the cheapest item in this report.
3
Scope the teacher inquiry. Define the sample — including non-users and lapsed users — and commission the implementation-research design expertise the study needs.

Before the platform build

4
Inventory the crosswalks. Establish which curricula are covered and how requests are currently received, since this is the only distribution channel for the artifact the model depends on.
5
Specify the telemetry against the model. Once the intervention specification exists, write the logging requirement from it — what event, at what grain, answering which link in the chain — and take that to the product roadmap with an owner and a date.

About This Report

This report has two purposes: first, to articulate a working model of the mechanisms through which SELweb is intended to produce impact; and second, to identify where measurement would most improve xSEL Labs' ability to understand and demonstrate that impact.

  1. Articulate a working model of how SELweb is intended to produce impact — from the assessment through teacher behavior to student outcomes.
  2. Use that model to identify and prioritize opportunities for impact measurement.

Our aim is to help xSEL Labs identify opportunities to better measure the intended impact of the assessments on teaching and learning.
